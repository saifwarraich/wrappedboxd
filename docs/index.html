Everything it tracks

Eight ways to look at what you watch.

Every tab in the panel, pulled from your own Letterboxd export and enriched with cast, crew, genre, and keyword data.

01

Overview

Films logged, hours watched, average rating, countries, languages, this year, rewatches, at a glance.

02

Directors & actors

Top watched, with photos, film counts, and your average rating for their work. Click through to every film.

03

Genres

A breakdown by total, plus a stacked view of how your taste shifted year over year.

04

Themes

A tag cloud of TMDB keywords: the ideas and motifs that show up again and again in what you watch.

05

Decades

Every film sorted by the decade it was made, from the 1920s to now.

06

Languages

Original spoken languages across your library, ranked and clickable.

07

Ratings

Your rating distribution, plus how your scores compare to TMDB's average for the same films.

08

Filters

Slice every section by year watched, decade, or genre, and the whole panel updates together.

Setup

No API keys. Just your export.

Letterboxd doesn't have a public API, so the panel is built from the export you already have access to, and enrichment happens automatically once you upload it.

1

Export your data

From Letterboxd settings, download your archive. It contains watched.csv, ratings.csv, and diary.csv.

2

Upload on your profile

Visit your own Letterboxd profile, and the extension walks you through uploading the files, right where the panel lives.

3

It stays current

Every visit quietly syncs your RSS feed for new entries. Re-upload anytime to catch up unlogged watches.

Where your data lives

Local by default.

●

Your film data stays in your browser. Everything from your CSV export and RSS sync is stored locally in IndexedDB on your machine. WrappedBoxd doesn't run a backend or database of its own. The only outside calls are to TMDB, to fetch public poster, cast, and genre data for the films you've already watched.
